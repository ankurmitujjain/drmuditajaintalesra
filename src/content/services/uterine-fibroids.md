## What are uterine fibroids?

**Uterine fibroids (leiomyomas, रसौली) are benign growths that develop from the muscle wall of the uterus.** They are very common: many women develop fibroids by the age of 50, though a large number never know they have them because they cause no symptoms.

Fibroids are described by where they grow:

| Type | Location | Typical effect |
|---|---|---|
| Submucosal | Bulges into the uterine cavity | Heavy bleeding, difficulty conceiving, miscarriage |
| Intramural | Within the muscle wall | Heavy periods, pain, enlarged uterus |
| Subserosal | On the outer surface | Pressure on bladder or bowel, abdominal swelling |
| Pedunculated | Attached by a stalk | Occasional sudden pain if twisted |

## What symptoms do fibroids cause?

Symptoms depend on the size, number and position of the fibroids. Common complaints include:

- **Heavy or prolonged periods**, sometimes with clots
- **Anaemia**: tiredness, breathlessness, low haemoglobin
- **Pelvic pain or pressure**, or a feeling of heaviness
- **Frequent urination** or constipation from pressure
- **Abdominal swelling**
- **Difficulty conceiving or recurrent miscarriage** (mainly submucosal fibroids)
- **Pain during intercourse**

## When should you see a gynaecologist?

Book a consultation if your periods have become heavier or longer, you soak a pad every 1–2 hours, you feel tired or breathless (possible anaemia), you notice a swelling in the lower abdomen, or you have been trying to conceive without success. **Seek urgent care for very heavy bleeding with dizziness or fainting.**

## How are fibroids diagnosed?

1. **Clinical history and pelvic examination.**
2. **Pelvic ultrasound** (abdominal and transvaginal): the main test to map the number, size and location of fibroids.
3. **Blood tests**: haemoglobin for anaemia, and other tests as needed.
4. **MRI pelvis**, in selected cases, to plan complex surgery.
5. **Hysteroscopy**, to look inside the uterine cavity if a submucosal fibroid or polyp is suspected.

## What are the treatment options for fibroids?

Treatment is personalised. It depends on your symptoms, age, the fibroids' size and location, and whether you want to have children in future.

### 1. Watchful waiting
Small fibroids without symptoms are monitored with periodic ultrasound. No treatment is needed unless symptoms develop.

### 2. Medical treatment
- **Medicines to reduce bleeding and pain** (for example tranexamic acid and anti-inflammatory medicines), taken during periods.
- **Hormonal treatments** such as the levonorgestrel intrauterine system (hormonal IUD) or other hormonal medicines, which can control bleeding in suitable women.
- **Short-term medicines that shrink fibroids** before surgery, or to correct anaemia beforehand.
- **Iron supplements** to correct anaemia.

Medicines control symptoms but generally do not remove fibroids permanently.

### 3. Laparoscopic myomectomy (keyhole fibroid removal)
For women who want to keep their uterus, **laparoscopic myomectomy** removes the fibroids through small cuts of about 5–10 mm. The uterine wall is then carefully repaired in layers. Compared with open surgery, the benefits usually include less pain, a shorter hospital stay, smaller scars and a faster return to normal life.

### 4. Hysteroscopic myomectomy
Submucosal fibroids inside the uterine cavity can often be removed through the vagina with a hysteroscope (a thin telescope), with no cuts on the abdomen.

### 5. Hysterectomy
When family is complete and symptoms are severe, or fibroids are very large or numerous, removing the uterus (hysterectomy) gives a permanent solution. It can usually be done laparoscopically. See [Laparoscopic Hysterectomy](/laparoscopic-hysterectomy/).

## Myomectomy vs hysterectomy: which is right for you?

| | Myomectomy | Hysterectomy |
|---|---|---|
| Uterus | Preserved | Removed |
| Future pregnancy | Possible | Not possible |
| Periods | Continue | Stop |
| Chance of new fibroids | Possible | None |
| Best for | Women wanting fertility or to keep the uterus | Family complete, severe or recurrent fibroids |

The decision is always made together, after discussing your priorities, risks and benefits.

## Fibroids and pregnancy

Most women with fibroids have normal pregnancies. Sometimes fibroids grow during pregnancy and cause pain, or are linked with miscarriage, preterm labour, abnormal baby position or a higher chance of caesarean delivery. If you have fibroids and are planning a pregnancy, a pre-pregnancy consultation helps decide whether treatment is needed first. See [Pregnancy Care](/obstetrics-services/).
