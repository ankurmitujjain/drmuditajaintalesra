## What is laparoscopic hysterectomy?

**Laparoscopic hysterectomy is the surgical removal of the uterus (बच्चेदानी) using a laparoscope**, a thin telescope with a camera, inserted through 3–4 small cuts of 5–10 mm. The uterus is usually removed through the vagina, so there is **no large abdominal scar**.

Types of hysterectomy include:

| Type | What is removed |
|---|---|
| Total hysterectomy | Uterus and cervix |
| Subtotal hysterectomy | Uterus only; the cervix is preserved |
| Hysterectomy with salpingectomy | Uterus and fallopian tubes (ovaries preserved) |
| Hysterectomy with oophorectomy | Uterus and one or both ovaries |

**Total Laparoscopic Hysterectomy (TLH)** is the most common minimally invasive approach.

## When is hysterectomy recommended?

- **Uterine fibroids** causing heavy bleeding, pain or pressure, when family is complete (see [Fibroid Treatment](/uterine-fibroids/))
- **Adenomyosis** with severe pain and bleeding
- **Heavy menstrual bleeding** not responding to medicines or a hormonal IUD
- **Uterine prolapse** (often treated by the vaginal route; see [Vaginal Surgery](/vaginal-surgery-services/))
- **Endometrial hyperplasia** or **precancerous cervical changes**, in selected cases
- **Chronic pelvic pain** from specific uterine causes

**Hysterectomy is not the first answer.** Alternatives such as medicines, the hormonal IUD, hysteroscopic procedures and myomectomy are always considered and discussed first.

## Laparoscopic vs open (abdominal) hysterectomy

| | Laparoscopic | Open abdominal |
|---|---|---|
| Incision | 3–4 small cuts (5–10 mm) | One large cut (10–15 cm) |
| Pain after surgery | Usually less | Usually more |
| Hospital stay | About 1–3 days | Often longer |
| Return to routine | About 2–4 weeks | About 6–8 weeks |
| Scar | Minimal | Visible abdominal scar |
| Infection / hernia risk | Lower | Higher |

Some situations, such as a very large uterus or extensive previous surgery, may still need open surgery. This is assessed before the operation.

## How should you prepare for surgery?

1. **Consultation and examination**, with an ultrasound (and MRI if needed)
2. **Pap smear / cervical screening** and an endometrial assessment when indicated
3. **Blood tests** and pre-anaesthesia check-up; correction of anaemia before surgery
4. **Detailed discussion** of the ovaries and tubes, risks, benefits and recovery
5. **Written informed consent**

## What happens on the day of surgery?

- Performed under **general anaesthesia**
- Usually takes about 1.5–3 hours, depending on uterine size and any adhesions
- The bladder, ureters and bowel are carefully protected
- The uterus is removed through the vagina, and the vaginal vault is closed with stitches

## Recovery after laparoscopic hysterectomy

| Timeline | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Day of surgery | Walking within hours; liquids, then a light diet |
| Days 1–3 | Discharge home; mild pain controlled with tablets |
| Weeks 1–2 | Light household activity; short walks |
| Weeks 2–4 | Most women return to desk work |
| Week 6 | Follow-up check; gradual return to exercise and intercourse |

**Contact the clinic** if you have fever, heavy bleeding, foul-smelling discharge, increasing pain or difficulty passing urine.

## What are the risks?

As with any surgery, there are risks: bleeding, infection, injury to the bladder, ureters or bowel (uncommon), blood clots, and reactions to anaesthesia. In a small number of cases, the surgeon may need to convert to open surgery for safety. These risks are explained personally before you decide.
