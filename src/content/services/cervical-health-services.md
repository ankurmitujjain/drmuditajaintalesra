## Eradicating Cervical Cancer Through Prevention

Cervical cancer remains one of the leading causes of cancer-related mortality among women in India. Approximately **120,000 Indian women are diagnosed with cervical cancer annually**, and nearly **75,000 succumb** to the disease—largely because it is detected at advanced, symptomatic stages.

The tragedy of these statistics is that **cervical cancer is almost completely preventable**. The causative agent—high-risk oncogenic strains of the **Human Papillomavirus (HPV)**—requires 10 to 15 years of persistent infection before progressing from cellular dysplasia to invasive cancer.

## 1. Preventive HPV Vaccination (The Cervical Vaccine)

HPV vaccines stimulate high titers of neutralizing antibodies against the high-risk viral strains responsible for over 70% to 90% of cervical cancers, as well as genital warts:
- **Recommended Age Groups:**
  - *Ages 9–14 Years:* Two-dose schedule (months 0 and 6). Offers the strongest immune response.
  - *Ages 15–26 Years:* Three-dose schedule (months 0, 1–2, and 6).
  - *Ages 27–45 Years:* Catch-up vaccination remains clinically beneficial following consultation.
- **Safety Profile:** Approved worldwide by WHO, DCGI, and FOGSI, with an exemplary safety profile across hundreds of millions of administered doses globally.

## 2. In-Clinic Cervical Screening Protocols

Early detection identifies abnormal cervical epithelial changes before they become cancerous:

| Screening Modality | Testing Frequency | Diagnostic Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Liquid-Based Cytology (LBC Pap Test)** | Every 3 years (ages 21–65) | Collects exfoliated cells from the cervical squamocolumnar junction to detect cellular atypia (ASCUS, LSIL, HSIL). |
| **High-Risk HPV DNA Testing** | Every 5 years (ages 25–65) | Molecular PCR test detecting genetic material of 14 high-risk HPV strains (including HPV 16 and 18). |
| **Co-Testing (Pap + HPV DNA)** | Every 5 years (preferred over 30) | Highest diagnostic sensitivity (>95%) for cervical precancer detection. |

## 3. Managing Abnormal Pap Smear Results

An abnormal Pap smear does not mean you have cancer. It signifies that atypical cells were observed that require closer investigation:
- **Colposcopy:** An illuminated microscopic examination of the cervix using dilute acetic acid and Lugol's iodine to highlight dysplastic epithelium.
- **Directed Cervical Biopsy:** Painless micro-sampling of suspicious areas for histopathological diagnosis.
- **Excisional Treatments (LEEP / LLETZ):** Simple outpatient loop electrosurgical excision that removes precancerous dysplastic tissue, halting cancer progression while preserving cervical function.
