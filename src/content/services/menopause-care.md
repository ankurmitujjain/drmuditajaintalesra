## What is menopause?

**Menopause is the time when the ovaries stop releasing eggs and periods end permanently.** It is confirmed after **12 consecutive months without a period**. The transition before it, called **perimenopause**, can last several years, with irregular periods and fluctuating symptoms.

| Stage | What happens |
|---|---|
| Perimenopause | Irregular periods, symptoms begin; pregnancy is still possible |
| Menopause | 12 months without a period |
| Postmenopause | The years after menopause; long-term bone and heart health become the focus |

Menopause can also happen earlier after surgical removal of both ovaries, or after some cancer treatments.

## What are the common symptoms?

- **Hot flashes and night sweats**
- **Sleep problems**
- **Mood changes**, irritability, anxiety or low mood
- **Brain fog** and poor concentration
- **Vaginal dryness**, discomfort and painful intercourse
- **Urinary urgency** or recurrent urinary infections
- **Joint aches**, fatigue and weight changes
- Reduced libido

Every woman's experience is different. Some have few symptoms; others find daily life significantly affected.

## When should you see a gynaecologist?

- Symptoms that affect your sleep, work, relationships or quality of life
- **Any bleeding after menopause** (12 months without periods). Get this checked promptly.
- Periods stopping **before age 40–45**
- Heavy or very frequent bleeding during perimenopause
- Concerns about bone health, heart health or cancer screening

## What does a menopause consultation include?

1. **Symptom and health review**, including family history of heart disease, osteoporosis and cancers
2. **Examination**: blood pressure, weight, breast and pelvic examination
3. **Screening tests**, as appropriate: blood sugar, lipids, thyroid, vitamin D, **Pap smear / HPV test**, mammography and a **bone density (DEXA) scan**
4. **A personalised treatment plan**

## What are the treatment options?

### 1. Lifestyle measures
- Regular exercise, including strength and weight-bearing exercise for bones
- A diet rich in calcium, protein, vegetables and whole grains; limited sugar and alcohol
- Dressing in layers, keeping cool, and avoiding triggers such as spicy food and caffeine
- Good sleep habits, yoga and stress management
- Stopping smoking

### 2. Menopausal hormone therapy (MHT / HRT)
The most effective treatment for hot flashes and night sweats. It also protects bone. It is available as tablets, patches or gels, with or without progesterone depending on whether you have a uterus. It is prescribed **after an individual risk assessment**, at the lowest effective dose, with regular review.

### 3. Non-hormonal treatment
Certain non-hormonal prescription medicines can reduce hot flashes for women who can't take hormones. Calcium, vitamin D and bone-protecting medicines are used when indicated.

### 4. Vaginal and urinary symptoms
Vaginal moisturisers, lubricants and **low-dose vaginal oestrogen** work well and are safe for most women. Pelvic floor exercises help with urinary leakage. See [Vaginal & Pelvic Floor Surgery](/vaginal-surgery-services/) for prolapse care.

## Menopause treatment at a glance

| Concern | Options |
|---|---|
| Hot flashes / night sweats | Lifestyle changes, MHT, non-hormonal medicines |
| Vaginal dryness / painful sex | Moisturisers, lubricants, vaginal oestrogen |
| Bone health | Calcium, vitamin D, exercise, DEXA scan, medicines if needed |
| Mood / sleep | Lifestyle changes, MHT where suitable, counselling |
| Bleeding after menopause | Prompt ultrasound and endometrial evaluation |
