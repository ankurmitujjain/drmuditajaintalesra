## What is an ovarian cyst?

**An ovarian cyst is a fluid-filled sac that forms in or on an ovary.** Ovarian cysts are very common, especially during the reproductive years, and are often found by chance on an ultrasound done for another reason.

## What types of ovarian cysts are there?

| Type | What it is | Usual course |
|---|---|---|
| Follicular cyst | A follicle that didn't release its egg | Usually resolves in 1–3 cycles |
| Corpus luteum cyst | Persists after ovulation; may bleed | Usually resolves |
| Haemorrhagic cyst | A functional cyst with bleeding inside | Usually resolves; can be painful |
| Endometrioma ("chocolate cyst") | Caused by endometriosis | May need treatment |
| Dermoid cyst (mature teratoma) | Contains tissues such as fat or hair | Usually removed surgically |
| Cystadenoma | Benign cyst from the ovary's surface cells | Often removed if large |
| Polycystic ovaries | Many small follicles (PCOS), not true cysts | Managed medically |

## What symptoms do ovarian cysts cause?

Many cysts cause **no symptoms**. When they do, symptoms may include:

- Dull or sharp **pain on one side** of the lower abdomen
- **Bloating** or a feeling of fullness
- **Pain during intercourse**
- Changes in periods
- Frequent urination from pressure

**Go to a hospital immediately** for sudden severe pelvic pain, especially with vomiting, fever, dizziness or fainting. This may be **ovarian torsion** (twisting) or a ruptured cyst, both of which need urgent treatment.

## How are ovarian cysts evaluated?

1. **Pelvic ultrasound** (transvaginal and abdominal): size, wall thickness, internal features and blood flow. Standardised systems such as **IOTA / O-RADS** help classify risk.
2. **Blood tests** when indicated: CA-125 (especially after menopause or with complex cysts), and other markers in younger women with solid-looking cysts.
3. **Repeat ultrasound** after 6–12 weeks for simple cysts, to confirm they have resolved.
4. **MRI** in selected cases.

## How are ovarian cysts treated?

### 1. Watchful waiting
Simple cysts under about 5 cm in women of reproductive age usually need only a repeat scan. Pain relief is given if needed.

### 2. Medical treatment
Hormonal pills may be advised to prevent new functional cysts in women who get them repeatedly. Endometriomas may be managed with hormonal treatment (see [Endometriosis Treatment](/endometriosis-treatment/)).

### 3. Laparoscopic ovarian cystectomy (keyhole surgery)
When surgery is needed, **laparoscopic cystectomy** is usually preferred:
- 3–4 small cuts of 5–10 mm
- The cyst is carefully separated and removed, **preserving healthy ovarian tissue**
- Less pain, a shorter hospital stay and a faster recovery than open surgery
- The cyst is sent for histopathology to confirm its nature

## Ovarian cysts in pregnancy

Small functional cysts are common in early pregnancy and usually resolve on their own. Larger or persistent cysts are monitored. Surgery, if needed, is typically planned for the second trimester. See [Pregnancy Care](/obstetrics-services/).

## Ovarian cysts after menopause

Any new cyst after menopause is evaluated carefully with ultrasound and CA-125. Many are simple and benign and are monitored. Complex cysts may need surgery, with referral to a gynae-oncology team when indicated.
