## The Three Diagnostic Pillars of Fertility Evaluation

Human reproduction relies on the harmonious interplay of multiple biological systems. A structured initial workup evaluates both partners simultaneously:

### 1. Ovarian Reserve and Ovulation (Female Factor)
Approximately 25% to 30% of subfertility involves ovulatory dysfunction:
- **Anti-Müllerian Hormone (AMH):** A stable blood biomarker reflecting the remaining primordial follicle pool in the ovaries.
- **Transvaginal Pelvic Ultrasound:** Evaluates the Antral Follicle Count (AFC), checks for polycystic ovarian morphology, and assesses uterine cavity health (ruling out endometrial polyps, submucosal fibroids, or adenomyosis).
- **Hormonal Baseline Panel:** Day 2/3 FSH, LH, Estradiol, Serum Prolactin, and Thyroid Stimulating Hormone (TSH).

### 2. Fallopian Tube Patency and Pelvic Anatomy
Fertilization occurs within the ampulla of the fallopian tube. If the tubes are blocked or trapped by adhesions, sperm cannot reach the egg:
- **Hysterosalpingography (HSG):** A specialized fluoroscopic radiopaque contrast dye test outlining the uterine cavity and demonstrating peritoneal spill through both fallopian tubes.
- **Diagnostic Laparoscopy & Chromopertubation:** Considered the gold standard. Under direct high-definition visualization, dilute methylene blue dye is injected through the cervix to evaluate bilateral tubal spill, while simultaneously diagnosing and treating mild-to-moderate endometriosis or peritubal adhesions.

### 3. Semen Quality Assessment (Male Factor)
Male reproductive health contributes to approximately 40% to 50% of conception challenges. A comprehensive semen analysis evaluated according to the latest **World Health Organization (WHO)** laboratory manual assesses:
- Semen Volume ($\ge 1.4	ext{ mL}$)
- Total Sperm Count ($\ge 16	ext{ million/mL}$)
- Total Motility ($\ge 42\%$) and Progressive Motility ($\ge 30\%$)
- Normal Sperm Morphology ($\ge 4\%$)

## Graduated Treatment Modalities Offered

Treatment protocols follow a logical, step-by-step ladder tailored to the couple’s specific diagnostic findings:

### 1. Medical Ovulation Induction & Timed Intercourse
Ideal for women with anovulatory cycles, particularly those with PCOS:
- Oral ovulatory medications (such as Letrozole or Clomiphene Citrate) stimulate mature ovarian follicle development.
- **Serial Follicular Sonography:** Tracks follicle growth from day 9 to day 14. Once the leading follicle achieves 18 mm to 20 mm with a trilaminar endometrium ($\ge 8	ext{ mm}$), an hCG trigger injection is administered to induce timed ovulation, optimizing the window for intercourse.

### 2. Intrauterine Insemination (IUI)
A gentle, non-invasive assisted reproductive technique:
- Semen is collected and processed in the laboratory to eliminate seminal plasma, prostaglandins, and non-motile debris, concentrating the healthiest motile sperm in a nutrient-rich culture medium.
- Using a soft, flexible catheter, the concentrated sperm suspension is placed directly into the uterine fundus, bypassing the cervical barrier and placing millions of active sperm in close proximity to the fallopian tubes.

### 3. Minimally Invasive Fertility-Enhancing Surgery
When mechanical or structural barriers are identified:
- Laparoscopic ovarian drilling for medication-resistant PCOS.
- Laparoscopic excision of endometriomas and adhesiolysis to restore pelvic mobile anatomy.
- Hysteroscopic resection of uterine septa, polyps, or intrauterine synechiae (Asherman's syndrome).
