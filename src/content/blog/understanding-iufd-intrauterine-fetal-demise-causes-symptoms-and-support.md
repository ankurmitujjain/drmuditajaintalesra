## A Sensitive Clinical Subject Handled with Utmost Empathy

The unexpected loss of a pregnancy in the second or third trimester is an emotionally heartbreaking experience for parents. Medical care must pair the highest level of compassionate bereavement support with rigorous medical investigation to understand contributing factors and safeguard future pregnancies.

---

## Known Clinical Contributing Factors

1. **Placental Complications:** Abruptio placentae (placental detachment) or severe placental insufficiency restricting oxygen delivery.
2. **Umbilical Cord Accidents:** Cord prolapse, tight true knots, or constriction restricting fetal perfusion.
3. **Maternal Health Conditions:** Uncontrolled gestational diabetes, severe preeclampsia, antiphospholipid syndrome (thrombophilia), or systemic infections.
4. **Fetal Genetic or Structural Anomalies:** Chromosomal abnormalities or major congenital heart malformations.

---

## The Critical Importance of Daily Kick Counts

Every mother should be attentive to fetal movements from week 28 onward:
- Count movements at the same time each day, preferably after eating.
- If you notice a sudden drop in movement intensity or frequency, **contact your obstetrician immediately**. Early Doppler and ultrasound evaluation can be life-saving.
