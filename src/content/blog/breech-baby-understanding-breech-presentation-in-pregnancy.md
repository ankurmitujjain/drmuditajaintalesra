## What is Breech Presentation?

In a typical pregnancy, most babies settle into a head-down (cephalic or vertex) position between 32 and 36 weeks of gestation in preparation for birth. In approximately 3% to 4% of full-term pregnancies, the fetus remains bottom-first or feet-first—a condition known as **breech presentation**.

Dr. Mudita Jain Talesra, senior obstetrician in Bhopal, explains the causes, diagnostic milestones, and delivery considerations for expectant mothers.

## Types of Breech Presentation

- **Frank Breech:** The baby's buttocks lead into the pelvis with legs pointing straight up toward the head. This is the most common variation (50%–70%).
- **Complete Breech:** The baby is sitting cross-legged with buttocks and feet presenting together.
- **Footling Breech:** One or both feet point downward and would be born first.

---

## Causes and Contributing Factors

A baby may remain in a breech position due to several maternal or fetal factors:
1. **Amniotic Fluid Volume:** Either too much fluid (polyhydramnios) allowing excessive movement, or too little (oligohydramnios) restricting rotation.
2. **Uterine Anatomy:** Presence of uterine fibroids or a septate/bicornuate uterus.
3. **Placental Location:** Placenta previa (low-lying placenta) blocking the lower uterine segment.
4. **Multiple Pregnancies:** Twin gestations where fetal maneuvering space is constrained.
