## The Wonders of Fetal Respiration

One of the most common questions expectant parents ask is: *If a baby is surrounded by fluid inside the amniotic sac, how does it get oxygen without drowning?*

The answer lies in the miraculous biological architecture of the placenta and umbilical cord, connecting the maternal circulatory system to fetal circulation without ever mixing their separate blood supplies.

---

## The Role of the Placenta and Umbilical Cord

- **Oxygen Exchange:** Maternal blood fills microscopic spaces in the placenta. Oxygen and nutrients diffuse across thin placental membranes into tiny fetal capillaries.
- **Umbilical Vein:** Carries freshly oxygenated, nutrient-rich blood from the placenta into the baby's heart and developing brain.
- **Umbilical Arteries:** Carry carbon dioxide and metabolic waste products back from the baby to the placenta, where the mother's lungs eliminate the carbon dioxide.

---

## Fetal Practice Breathing Movements

Starting around the end of the first trimester (weeks 10 to 12), the fetus begins rhythmic diaphragmatic and chest movements. These "practice breaths" expand lung tissue and circulate fluid through developing airways, ensuring the respiratory system is mature by term.
